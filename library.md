Each source carries a note saying what it establishes, so a reader can place it
before opening it. A source is listed here only once it has been opened at its
own address. Sources that are cited in the instrument but have not yet been read
are listed at the foot, under their own heading, and the instrument marks every
row drawn from them.

The sources are grouped as law and as guidance. The law binds. The guidance says
how a body reads what binds, which is a different thing and never appears as the
same kind of row.

## Law

### Regulation (EU) 2016/679, General Data Protection Regulation {#gdpr}
European Parliament and Council · European Union · 27 April 2016
https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32016R0679
Establishes: That unauthorised access to personal data is itself a breach; that the notification to the supervisory authority runs seventy-two hours from awareness; that the notification may be made in phases where the information cannot be given at once; and that the documentation obligation applies whether or not anything is notified.

Article 4(12) defines a personal data breach as a breach of security leading to
the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorised
disclosure of, or access to, personal data. Access is in the definition, so an
employee who opens records they had no business opening has caused a breach
without anything leaving the building.

Article 33(1) sets seventy-two hours from becoming aware, unless the breach is
unlikely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, and
requires reasons for the delay where the term is exceeded. Article 33(3) lists
what the notification must contain, qualifying the categories and approximate
numbers with "where possible". Article 33(4) allows the information to be given
in phases. Article 33(5) requires the facts, the effects and the remedial action
to be documented in every case.

It does not define the moment of awareness, which is the question the whole
instrument turns on.

### Directive (EU) 2022/2555, NIS2 {#nis2}
European Parliament and Council · European Union · 14 December 2022
https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32022L2555
Establishes: A cascade of five reporting acts from awareness of a significant incident, and that its incident reporting does not apply to entities covered by a sector-specific Union act of at least equivalent effect.

Article 23(4) requires an early warning within twenty-four hours of becoming
aware, an incident notification within seventy-two, an intermediate report on
request, a final report within one month of the notification, and a progress
report where the incident is still open. Each stage states what it must contain.

Article 4(1) is the reason a bank does not appear under this Directive at all:
where a sector-specific Union act requires notification of significant incidents
and its requirements are at least equivalent in effect, the relevant provisions
of the Directive do not apply to those entities.

### Regulation (EU) 2022/2554, DORA {#dora}
European Parliament and Council · European Union · 14 December 2022
https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32022R2554
Establishes: That a major ICT-related incident is reported in three stages, that a significant credit institution reports to its national authority rather than to the ECB, and that clients are told where the incident touches their financial interests.

Article 19(1) sends the report to the competent authority under Article 46, and
its third subparagraph sends the report of a significant credit institution to
the national competent authority, which transmits it to the ECB immediately.
Supervision moves under the Single Supervisory Mechanism; the report does not.

Article 19(3) requires clients to be informed, without undue delay, where the
incident has an impact on their financial interests. Article 19(4) names the
initial notification, the intermediate report and the final report but sets no
hours: it refers them to technical standards under Article 20.

### Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/301 {#dora-rts-terms}
European Commission · European Union · 23 October 2024
https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32025R0301
Establishes: The hours DORA leaves to technical standards, and a formal act for a term the entity knows it will miss.

Article 5(1) requires the initial notification as early as possible, within four
hours of classifying the incident as major and no later than twenty-four hours
from becoming aware of it; the intermediate report within seventy-two hours of
that notification, whether or not anything has changed; and the final report no
later than one month after the intermediate one. Article 5(2) covers the case
where the incident is classified as major only later: four hours from the
classification.

Article 5(3) is the provision a practitioner should know exists. An entity that
cannot submit within a term must tell the competent authority before that term
expires and give the reasons.

### Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/1772 {#dora-rts-class}
European Commission · European Union · 13 March 2024
https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024R1772
Establishes: When an ICT-related incident is major, which is the gate before any DORA term begins to run.

Article 8 makes an incident major where it has affected critical services and
either the reputational materiality threshold is met, or two or more of the
other thresholds are. Article 9 sets those thresholds. Recurring incidents that
are individually minor count as one major incident where they occur at least
twice in six months with the same apparent root cause and together meet the
criteria.

### Council Regulation (EU) No 1024/2013, SSM Regulation {#ssm}
Council of the European Union · European Union · 15 October 2013
https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32013R1024
Establishes: When a credit institution is significant, which changes who supervises it and, under DORA, what happens to its incident report after it arrives.

Article 6(4) assesses significance on size, importance for the economy of the
Union or a participating Member State, and the significance of cross-border
activities. An institution is not treated as less significant where its assets
exceed thirty billion euro, where its assets exceed twenty per cent of the GDP
of its Member State and are above five billion, or where the national authority
proposes it and the ECB confirms after a comprehensive assessment.

## Guidance

### Guidelines 9/2022 on personal data breach notification under GDPR {#edpb-9-2022}
European Data Protection Board · European Union · 4 April 2023, version 2.0
https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2023-04/edpb_guidelines_202209_personal_data_breach_notification_v2.0_en.pdf
Establishes: How the Board reads the moment of awareness, that a short period of investigation precedes it, and that an unbounded one can be treated as a failure to notify.

Paragraph 31 puts awareness at a reasonable degree of certainty that a security
incident has occurred that has led to personal data being compromised.
Paragraph 34 allows a short period of investigation to establish whether a
breach has in fact occurred, during which the controller may not be regarded as
aware. Paragraph 36 expects those preliminary actions to be completed soon after
the initial alert and says a longer period should be exceptional.

Paragraph 37 names the technical measures the Board has in mind: data flow and
log analysers, from which events and alerts are defined by correlating log data.
Paragraph 39 expects security events to reach a person whose task is
establishing that a breach exists and assessing the risk. Paragraph 40 states
the consequence of drift: where a controller fails to act in time and it becomes
apparent that a breach did occur, that can be treated as a failure to notify
under Article 33. Paragraph 41 treats the ability to detect, address and report
a breach in a timely manner as an element of the Article 32 measures.

This is guidance. It does not impose the standard it describes; it says how the
Board reads a provision that does.

### Guidelines 01/2021 on Examples regarding Personal Data Breach Notification {#edpb-1-2021}
European Data Protection Board · European Union · 14 December 2021, version 2.0
https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/2022-01/edpb_guidelines_012021_pdbnotification_adopted_en.pdf
Establishes: Eighteen worked cases, each with the facts, the risk assessment and the three outcomes, including one where the actor is an employee.

Case 8 is an employee who copies client contact data during his notice period
and uses it months later to approach those clients for his own business. No
special categories, low to medium volume, the database left intact. On those
facts the Board still refuses to call the risk low, at paragraph 74, because the
controller has no reassurance about the intentions of the employee and more
serious abuse is not ruled out. The outcome at paragraph 77: documentation
required, notification to the authority required, communication to the people
concerned not required.

Paragraph 72 says these breaches may be the hardest of all to prevent, because
doing the job requires the access that was misused.

It fixes reference points, not rules. Change the sensitivity, the volume or the
evidence of onward use and the second line moves.
